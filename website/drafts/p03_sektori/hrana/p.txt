Photorealistic black-and-white documentary photograph, summer 1962, Leica on Kodak Tri-X film, silver gelatin print, natural fine grain, true photographic realism, natural late-afternoon light, no illustration, no painting.
A vast wheat estate in the flat plains of Slavonia, eastern Croatia, at harvest. Wide shot, horizon low, large calm sky in the upper third for text.
In the middle distance two early-1960s combine harvesters work naturally in DIFFERENT parts of the field, at different angles, leaving cut stubble behind them; a truck drives along a dirt track toward a concrete grain silo complex on the horizon. The nearer combine is driven by a clearly visible cyborg operator in the open cab: polished chrome head and chrome arms on the controls, a natural part of the work.
In the foreground, at realistic scale, at the edge of the field: an old carved stone boundary marker stands in the grass between two plots; the estate owner, a weathered man in shirtsleeves, and a lawyer in a light summer suit holding a slim leather folder stand beside it in conversation, looking out over the land; the owner rests his hand on the boundary stone. Natural, unposed, real faces.
Selective color, delicate like a hand-tinted photograph: ONLY the ripe standing wheat is a soft, muted natural golden ochre. Everything else black and white. No text anywhere.
